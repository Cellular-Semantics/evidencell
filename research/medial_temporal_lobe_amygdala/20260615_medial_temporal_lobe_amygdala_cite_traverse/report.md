## Transcriptomic taxonomy and molecular identity of amygdala neurons

The most comprehensive molecular framework comes from single-cell transcriptomics of the mouse amygdala, which resolves the three principal neurotransmitter classes (Results-section primary evidence, [3]). The taxonomy identifies 130 neuronal cell types partitioned by neurotransmitter identity:

> "56 GABAergic cell types (Gad1, Gad2 and Slc32a1, 13,006 cells), 32 VGLUT1 types (Slc17a7, 11,947 cells) and 42 VGLUT2 types (Slc17a6, 5,232 cells)." [3]

These classes are nested within a structural-developmental partition that explains why CeA is GABAergic-dominated while BLA is glutamatergic-dominated:

> "The amygdala is a collection of anatomically and functionally defined nuclei belonging to the following three main structural-developmental regions: striatal, cortical and subplate amygdala nuclei." [3]

The taxonomy is functionally anchored — a subset of types responds transcriptionally to fear learning ([3]):

> "A subset of all neuronal types is transcriptionally responsive to fear learning and memory retrieval." [3]

The paper further argues that functionally distinct populations can be molecularly distinguished within the same circuit:

> "These alternate functions were often achieved through neuronal populations in the same regional circuitries, distinguished only by different molecular identities." [3]

Cross-species transcriptomics extends this framework to primates ([2], abstract):

> "Compositional analyses revealed that subdivisions of the primate basolateral complex contain distinct classes of glutamatergic neurons and divergent gene expression profiles for parvalbumin and somatostatin GABAergic neurons." [2]

> "Referencing primate neuron types to transcriptomic atlases of the murine amygdala highlighted primate-specific specializations for glutamatergic neurons and identified which neuron types are conserved across mammals and amygdalar subdivisions." [2]

## BLA principal neurons: the fear / extinction functional dichotomy

The functional partition of BLA glutamatergic principal neurons is stated directly ([1], Results):

> "the BLA projection neurons can be categorized as 'fear neurons' and 'extinction neurons' according to their activity in response to the conditioning stimulus." [1]

For the **extinction neuron**, the strongest molecular handle is Thy1. Optogenetic silencing of Thy1+ BLA neurons confirmed their fear-inhibiting role ([11]):

> "Selective inhibition of Thy1 neurons appears to allow maintenance of activity of the previously silenced Fear-On circuitry." [11]

That study also used cell-type-specific transcriptomics across the amygdala A-P axis ([11]):

> "The use of cell-type specific whole genome expression analysis allows in-depth interrogation of the molecular identity of a neural population of interest." [11]

This supports the Thy1 marker but does not, in the retrieved snippets, validate the other listed extinction markers (Ntsr2, Dkk3, Rspo2, Wnt7a). For the **fear neuron**, no distinct molecular marker emerges from the retrieved evidence — consistent with the node description (functionally defined by CS+ activation).

Anatomical context ([8], Introduction):

> "The LA and BLA represent the input to the amygdala and exhibit cortex-like characteristics with a majority of glutamatergic projection neurons." [8]

A review quantifies composition ([4]):

> "Developmentally, the BLA is a cortical-like structure consisting of 80% glutamatergic principal neurons and approximately 20% GABAergic inhibitory neurons." [4]

## BLA GABAergic interneurons: NPY+ neurogliaform and Calb1+ dendrite-targeting cells

The strongest direct evidence for the **NPY+ neurogliaform cell** is Vereczki et al. 2021 ([6]). NPY is established as the neurogliaform marker and the genetic entry point (Results):

> "As NPY has been shown to be a characteristic marker for the vast majority, if not for all neurogliaform cells in cortical regions, we used Npy-Cre mice to label these GABAergic neurons in the amygdala." [6]

Morphology and a negative-marker profile are documented:

> "Labeled interneurons in this group displayed characteristic morphological features of neurogliaform cells, including short smooth dendrites that often ramified and dense local axonal arborization." [6]

> "Many of these interneurons showed weak immunoreactivity for nNOS (22/25 tested), but none for PV (0/9 tested), CB (0/25 tested) or SST (0/4 tested)." [6]

Quantitatively ([6], abstract/Results):

> "NPY-containing neurogliaform cells (14%-15%)" [6]

with an estimate of the NPY+ subset that is neurogliaform:

> "neurogliaform cells may represent appr. 45 % of NPY+ GABAergic cells expressing EYFP but lacking immunoreactivity for PV, SST and CB." [6]

Corroborated in a review ([1], Introduction):

> "NPY-containing neurogliaform cells are 14-15 %, VIP and/or calretinin (CR)-expressing interneuron-selective interneurons are the most abundant at 29-38 %." [1]

For the **Calb1+ dendrite-targeting interneuron**, a review enumerates in-vivo LA-BLA interneuron classes ([1], Introduction):

> "This categorization resulted in four classes of LA-BLA interneurons: axo-axonic, CB+ (calbindin-positive) dendrite-targeting, AStria (amygdalo-striatal transition area), and PV+ (parvalbumin-positive)." [1]

A second source corroborates the dendrite-targeting function ([5], Results):

> "the Calb interneurons target the dendrites of the pyramidal cells and synchronize their activity to hypothalamic inputs." [5]

Functional fear-extinction relevance ([9], abstract):

> "GABAergic interneurons expressing calbindin, but not parvalbumin, were also activated by orexin-1 receptor antagonism in the basolateral amygdala." [9]

**Caveat on the Pvalb−/Calb1+ identity:** PV+ basket cells frequently co-express calbindin — "PV+ basket cells expressing calbindin (CB) innervate the soma and the proximal dendrites of principal neurons, whereas PV+ axo-axonic cells lacking CB form synaptic contacts on axon initial segments." [6] The target node is specifically the PV-negative dendrite-targeting subset and must be disambiguated from CB-co-expressing PV+ types.

## Central amygdala: striatum-like GABAergic organization

Developmental fate-mapping establishes the vLGE origin of CeA neurons ([12], abstract):

> "the CA appears to derive, at least in part, from progenitors located in the ventral lateral ganglionic eminence (vLGE)" [12]

with cellular consequence ([12], Introduction):

> "the CA contains striatum-like GABAergic projection neurons and represents the major output of the amygdala." [12]

A review confirms ([4]):

> "In contrast, CeA is a striatum-like structure that primarily comprises GABAergic inhibitory neurons." [4]

Background reviews note the CeL/CeM composition ([7], contextual):

> "CEl and CEm mainly contain GABAergic neurons." [7]

No retrieved evidence resolves the *aspiny morphology* distinction (large vs small soma) at the molecular level. The electrophysiology review describes CeA physiology by neuropeptide identity rather than soma size ([1], Introduction):

> "For CeA, SST+ and CRF+ neurons are characterized with early-spiking and late-firing phenotypes" [1]

— contextually useful, but not a marker for the large/small aspiny classes as defined here.

## Paralaminar immature excitatory neurons

The defining study describes a persistent immature population ([5], abstract):

> "The PL contains a large population of immature excitatory neurons at birth, some of which may continue to be born from local progenitors." [5]

Protracted maturation is well documented ([5]):

> "These progenitors disappear rapidly in infancy, but the immature neurons persist throughout childhood and adolescent ages, indicating that they develop on a protracted timeline." [5]

> "PL maturation occurs predominately during late childhood and into puberty when sex hormone levels change." [5]

Molecular identity from human scRNA-seq ([5], Results):

> "Single cell RNA-sequencing of human amygdala samples from 4-15 years of age identified a cluster of immature neurons that differentially express many of the genes (e.g. DCX, BCL-2, MAP2) that were also identified by immunostaining, as well as novel genes (e.g. ROBO1, BCL11B, SOX11) that are related to ongoing neural developmental processes." [5]

Excitatory identity confirmed:

> "At 13 years of age, 54.4% of immature DCX+ cells express T-box brain 1 (TBR1), an excitatory neuron transcription factor" [5]

> "Only about 3% of all cells in the PL express the GABAergic marker GAD67." [5]
