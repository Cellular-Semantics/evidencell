## Introduction

Cerebellar stellate cells are GABAergic molecular layer interneurons (MLIs) that inhabit the outer molecular layer of the cerebellar cortex [UBERON:0002974], where they form inhibitory synapses on the distal dendritic shafts of Purkinje cells [1][2][3]. Together with basket cells — their close morphological relatives occupying the inner molecular layer — they constitute the two classical MLI populations providing feed-forward and lateral inhibition to Purkinje output, as reviewed in Brown et al. [1] and Briatore et al. [4]. The classical stellate/basket distinction rests on soma position and axonal targeting, not on a transcriptomic discontinuity: both share Pvalb and RORa expression, and no stellate-specific molecular marker has been established. This shared marker profile is the central challenge for transcriptomic mapping of this type.

  > The simple, well-defined, and stereotyped circuitry of the cerebellum makes it an ideal model system for studying synapse development and maintenance (Figure 1). ### 1149 CBX MLI Megf11 Gaba_1 · 🟡 MODERATE

Annotation transfer from the Kozareva 2021 MLI1_2 (stellate) source cluster to WMBv1 places nearly all stellate source cells (Coverage=0.999) in the 311 CBX MLI Megf11 Gaba subclass, with the supertype 1149 CBX MLI Megf11 Gaba_1 [CS20230722_SUPT_1149] emerging as the best supertype-level target (F1=0.795 in `at_run_20260709_kozareva_cerebellum_mmc_wmbv1`). Two of the three classical markers assessed at atlas precomputed expression — Pvalb (mean 10.51, cohort percentile 0.973 across child clusters) and Grid1 (mean 8.96, cohort percentile 0.955) — are CONSISTENT with the classical stellate profile; RORa is absent from atlas expression data and cannot be assessed. Cerebellar cortex location is supported at the child-cluster level (see cluster paragraph below; no region data are available on the supertype stub itself).

**Supporting evidence:**
- AT coverage of the MLI1_2 source group is 99.9% at supertype level, indicating that virtually all transcriptomically-labelled stellate cells in the Kozareva dataset land within this supertype.
- Pvalb (atlas mean 10.51) and Grid1 (atlas mean 8.96) are CONSISTENT defining markers on this supertype, with child-cluster coverage 1.000 for both (all child clusters express both genes).
- Stage A discovery placed SUPT_1149 rank 1 in a 50-member cerebellar GABAergic cohort (score 7 vs next-best 6).

**Marker evidence provenance:**
- **Pvalb:** Established by protein immunofluorescence in Brown et al. [1] as a marker of MLIs generally, shared with Purkinje cells. The atlas labels Pvalb as DEFINING on sibling supertype 1150 CBX MLI Megf11 Gaba_2 and consistently expresses it at high levels across the 311 MLI subclass. This is a subclass-level marker, not stellate-specific — concordance here reflects shared MLI identity rather than stellate-specific biology.
- **RORa:** Established by protein immunofluorescence in Brown et al. [1], shared with Purkinje cells and all MLIs. Absent from atlas precomputed expression for SUPT_1149 — data gap, not a marker contradiction.
- **Grid1:** Established by mRNA ISH and protein IHC across MLIs in Konno et al. [5] — expressed in molecular layer interneurons broadly. Not stellate-specific.
- **No stellate-specific marker is currently on the classical node.** Pvalb, RORa, and Grid1 are all MLI-broad or broader markers. Any atlas candidate housing MLIs will score positively on all three, limiting discriminability beyond the MLI subclass level.

**Concerns:**
- The supertype SUPT_1149 is shared by both stellate and basket morphological types. MLI1_1 (basket cells, n=10,998) also maps to CLUS_5188 within this supertype with high coverage, though at lower purity (0.34 per `at_run_20260709_kozareva_cerebellum_mmc_wmbv1` caveats). The supertype is therefore transcriptomically cross-cutting: both classical MLI morphological subtypes map here. This is what the biology predicts, given no known molecular discriminator between stellate and basket cells.
- Supertype stub lacks region data — location consistency derives from child-cluster evidence only.

**What would upgrade confidence:**
- Discovery and validation of a stellate-specific molecular marker (absent from basket cells), ideally validated at transcript level in morphology-confirmed stellate cells. This would enable a more specific cluster-level mapping with higher purity.
- A bridging patch-seq dataset targeting morphologically identified stellate cells (post-hoc morphological reconstruction + snRNA-seq) mapped directly to WMBv1 via MapMyCells — expected output: AnnotationTransferEvidence on this edge with source confirmed as morphology-verified stellate cells.

## Discussion

### Best candidate + caveats

**Primary mapping:** Cerebellar stellate cell → 5188 CBX MLI Megf11 Gaba_1 [CS20230722_CLUS_5188] at MODERATE confidence. Key support: annotation transfer (MLI1_2→CLUS_5188, F1=0.794) and marker concordance (Pvalb CONSISTENT, Grid1 CONSISTENT). Key caveats: purity of 0.659 due to basket cells sharing CLUS_5188; no stellate-specific marker exists to discriminate the two morphological types at transcriptomic resolution.

This classical type maps directly to the Cell Ontology term cerebellar stellate cell [[CL:0010010](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ols4/ontologies/cl/classes?obo_id=CL:0010010)].

The supertype 1149 CBX MLI Megf11 Gaba_1 [CS20230722_SUPT_1149] is the broader target that acknowledges the cross-cutting biology. Both basket cells (MLI1_1) and stellate cells (MLI1_2) from Kozareva et al. 2021 map to SUPT_1149 with high coverage — the supertype appears to be the atlas representation of the shared MLI1 transcriptomic class, with no morphological subtype resolution at this rank. CLUS_5188 within SUPT_1149 is the primary cluster for the stellate source (Coverage=0.999) and is the best available resolution in the current atlas, but it is not stellate-exclusive.

The fundamental biological finding from this mapping is that WMBv1 does not resolve the stellate/basket transcriptomic distinction at any level (cluster, supertype, or subclass). This is consistent with the classical literature noting that the stellate/basket division is a morphological continuum without a known molecular discriminator [4].

### Proposed experiments and follow-ups

1. **Patch-seq with morphological reconstruction:** Obtain patch-seq data from morphologically confirmed cerebellar stellate cells (post-hoc biocytin fill + morphological reconstruction to confirm upper molecular layer position and dendritic targeting). Map to WMBv1 via MapMyCells. Expected output: `AnnotationTransferEvidence` on edges to CLUS_5188 and SUPT_1149 with a morphology-verified source. Threshold: F1 ≥ 0.80 at CLUSTER level with purity approaching 1.0 for a stellate-only cohort would confirm a stellate-enriched subpopulation; if purity remains ~0.5–0.7, the morphological continuum interpretation is supported.

2. **Targeted literature search for stellate-specific markers:** Conduct a cite-traverse from Kozareva et al. 2021 (PMID 34616064) and Osorno et al. 2022 (PMID 35578131) for any marker distinguishing MLI1 stellate from MLI1 basket cells at transcript level.
